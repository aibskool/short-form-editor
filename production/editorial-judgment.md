# Editorial judgment

For each spoken beat, write the viewer question, visual job, important word and expected takeaway. Accept Brandon's statement as the story input; do not require corroboration or score claim accuracy. Reject images that are visually confusing or unrelated to the edit.

## Visuals and placement

Prefer a relevant captured action where supplied; author animated UI or status graphics for missing stages. Crop to the useful detail. Never put disclaimers, verification labels or production caveats over the reel.

Anchor changes to the important spoken word. Keep subject, payoff and relationship readable in the first frame. Change the view when the spoken beat or action changes; give relevant footage enough time to read. Animate every on-screen text/card/illustration and add purposeful transition effects at appropriate boundaries even when omitted from the assembly. Do not impose a cut interval.

Use supplied screen captures to carry spoken actions when they are clear. Independently mark the key spoken words with animated green/white editorial text throughout the reel; a visible capture and lower-third caption do not make those callouts unnecessary. Place them clear of source detail. Plain text can briefly mark a word and should animate in/out without an automatic square box or timer bar. Add compact animated icons or a two-stage status change when they help. Larger kinetic UI/diagrams should explain something and generally hold for two to three seconds; remove a rushed or redundant complex panel. Inspect frame sequences for entrance, any meaningful internal change and exit. Confirm transition pixels sweep the intended full frame and do not become a small corner flash. Brandon prefers sparse, quiet whooshes and occasional soft pops over electronic beeps or effects on every text cue; protect the words and use no background music.

A finished result can open the reel before the presenter if the source is identifiable and the original voice continues. Use directed punch-ins and screen-crop moves on spoken changes, without a fixed cut interval or zooming a dense UI into unreadability. Group sequential accomplishments with consistent check/number styling, revealing each completed state on its spoken cue. Keep graphics above the fixed lower third or in a compact side-card when they collide. For filmed screens, document that native recording quality requires new capture; for dark-on-dark A-roll, plan a physical rim or background light on a future shoot. Keep the CTA keyword visible and lightly animated within the spoken ending, not a padded silent second.

For Brandon's full reels, the approved last calibration export is the visual benchmark: a brief face hook, full-frame dark radial grid with large white heading and green kicker, staged positive/negative rows, timely scan/state movement, wide transition wipe, and separate low spoken captions. Use a substantive full-frame authored scene for a claim with enough reading time; do not substitute tiny checks or scattered badges for the calibration's hierarchy. Screen captures can occupy their own readable beats between authored sections. Match the visual job rather than copying the calibration's particular numbers, business names or timing.

## Review

Inspect the actual encoded output at phone size and at 1× with sound. Judge short exact spoken captions and larger claim-linked green/white editorial graphics independently: record which words must be read, when they become fully visible, their collisions with Brandon's face or source proof, voice continuity, absence of background music and SFX masking. Check animation of every on-screen text/card/illustration and purposeful visual transitions at selected boundaries. Keep signal measurements separate from listening and still checks separate from motion review. Repair failures and verify the current render hash. The last tightened calibration has Brandon's style approval. A new full-reel adaptation remains pending until he reviews that exact export against it.
