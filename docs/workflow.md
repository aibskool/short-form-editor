# From story to reel

1. **Discover visuals:** combine supplied footage and useful source material. Research may provide ideas and imagery, but claim accuracy and supporting evidence are not editing gates for Brandon's filmed reels.
2. **Ideate:** choose a specific audience benefit and visible mechanism. Decide what the viewer can receive at the end.
3. **Script:** use actual supplied voice samples. Separate spoken copy from edit notes and map each beat to a visual job.
4. **Build the resource:** create the promised prompts, checklist or guide before locking the CTA. Check links and the exact keyword.
5. **Film and index:** transcribe the batch, identify the correct take, preserve the real voice and review any pause cuts.
6. **Gather assets:** select supplied recordings and author animated graphics for missing visual stages. Keep an asset index for file identity, not claim verification.
7. **Edit:** make the first frame understandable, choose presenter, screen capture, illustrative footage or kinetic graphics by spoken beat, time lower-third spoken captions separately from larger green/white editorial graphics, and synchronize visuals and sound to the locked words. Animate every authored text/card/illustration and add purposeful transitions at appropriate boundaries even if the assembly omits them. No background music or disclaimer overlays.
8. **Review:** inspect the actual encoded output at phone size and normal speed with sound. Log both text systems, motion, reading holds, speech integrity, animation, transitions and effects; confirm no background music or disclaimer. Do not check claim accuracy or request supporting evidence. Compare the calibration with the three Brandon references and leave style approval pending until Brandon reviews it.
9. **Package:** retain the timeline, asset provenance, review record, credits and actual giveaway. Prepare matching Skool/ManyChat handoff copy.
10. **Deliver:** follow the user’s current publication instructions, check the destination, and verify the resulting page/resource/message separately.

Resume at the appropriate stage. A request to repair a caption does not require redoing the entire research pipeline. A changed speech edit does require retiming and rechecking dependent cues.

The director routes this process; it does not silently start a recurring monitor, publish content or claim a pending review passed.
